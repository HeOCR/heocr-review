# Usability check: ten minutes, phone and desktop

For the owner, alone, to compare the new design with the current one. Use a batch you know. Do each task on the current app first, then on the new build, on the Pixel and at the desk. A stopwatch and a scrap of paper are enough.

Write down for each task: time, wrong taps or keys, and anything you had to look for.

| # | Task | Device | What to watch | The new design passes if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Decide 20 lines in a row, answering honestly | Phone, one hand | Thumb travel; wrong taps; does anything jump between lines | No answer needed a second hand or a stretch; no wrong tap on a neighbouring answer; the crop and text stayed in place |
| 2 | Correct three lines that each need one letter fixed | Phone | Can you see the crop while typing; do you find Save without scrolling | The crop, the box and Save were visible together with the keyboard up, every time |
| 3 | Handle one flagged line and one misaligned line | Phone, then desktop | Is it clear why Exact is unavailable on the phone; how many steps to open the page and come back | You knew what to do without reading twice; the page opened and closed in one tap each |
| 4 | Decide 30 lines with the keyboard only | Desktop | Do you ever reach for the mouse; do you glance at the key caps | No mouse; the page beside the line was enough to judge context without pressing `P` |
| 5 | Turn on airplane mode, answer one line, turn it off | Phone | Is it obvious the answer was not saved; is it obvious when it was | You never doubted where the answer was; it was saved once the connection returned, and only once |

Then two questions for yourself:

1. After five minutes, which version were you less tired on, and why?
2. Did anything in the new design make you want to press Exact when you were not sure?

If the answer to the second is yes, say where: that is a bias to remove before volunteers see it.
